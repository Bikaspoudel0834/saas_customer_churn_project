# SaaS Customer Churn & Revenue Analysis
## Business Insights


## 1. Revenue Growth and MRR Trend

Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) grew strongly throughout the analysis period, increasing from only $237 in January 2023 to a peak of $887,800 in November 2025. By December 2025, MRR decreased slightly to $874,305.

This shows that the company experienced strong recurring-revenue growth over time, but the small decline at the end of 2025 may indicate increasing churn or slower customer growth. Management should monitor this change closely and focus on retaining high-value customers while continuing to acquire new subscriptions.


## 2. Churn Trend

Monthly subscription churn changed throughout the analysis period but became noticeably higher toward the end of 2025. The churn rate increased from 7.47% in September 2025 to 9.36% in October, 9.70% in November, and reached 11.53% in December 2025.

This rising churn is a major business concern because losing customers directly reduces recurring revenue and increases pressure on customer acquisition. The company should investigate why customers are leaving, especially during the final months of 2025, and focus on retention strategies such as better onboarding, stronger customer support, product improvements, and targeted offers for customers at risk of cancellation.



## 3. Revenue Concentration by Plan

Enterprise was the strongest revenue-generating plan, producing approximately $6.27 million in paid invoice value. It was followed by Business Annual at about $3.41 million and Business Monthly at about $2.17 million.

This shows that a large share of the company’s revenue depends on higher-value plans, especially Enterprise and Business subscriptions. Because these customers contribute so much revenue, the company should prioritize retention, account management, and customer success efforts for these plans. Losing even a small number of high-value customers could have a major impact on recurring revenue.



## 4. Cancellation Reasons

Price was the most common cancellation reason, accounting for 27.21% of ended subscriptions. Missing features was the second largest reason at 23.70%, followed by no longer needed at 19.22%, switched vendor at 19.00%, and non-payment at 10.87%.

This shows that both pricing and product value are important retention issues. The company should review whether customers clearly understand the value they receive from each plan, consider better pricing or upgrade options, and prioritize product features that customers request most often. Reducing cancellations related to price and missing features could significantly improve retention.



## 5. Revenue at Risk by Plan

Enterprise had the highest monthly recurring revenue at risk, with about $425,456 linked to ended subscriptions. Business Monthly was next at about $169,071, followed by Business Annual at about $123,590.

This shows that losing customers from higher-value plans can hurt the company a lot. The company should pay more attention to Enterprise and Business customers, understand why they leave, and try to keep them before they cancel. Protecting these plans can help the company keep more recurring revenue.



## 6. Acquisition Channel Performance

Organic was the strongest acquisition channel, creating about $4.19 million in paid invoice value. Paid search was second with about $2.97 million, followed by unknown at about $2.47 million and partner at about $2.14 million.

This shows that organic customers bring a large amount of revenue to the company. The company should continue investing in organic growth, but it should also study why partner customers have a high average paid value. This can help the company understand which channels bring better customers and where marketing money should be used.



## 7. Churn by Industry

Media had the highest ended-subscription rate at about 59.54%. Nonprofit was next at about 54.34%, followed by government at about 53.85% and manufacturing at about 53.54%.

This shows that some industries are losing customers faster than others. The company should study the needs of media and other high-churn industries to understand what problems they are facing. Better support, pricing, or product features for these industries may help reduce churn.




## 8. Cohort Retention

Cohort retention results showed that customer retention changes a lot between different signup groups. Some cohorts kept more customers after 3, 6, and 12 months, while other cohorts lost customers faster.

This shows that customer experience may be different depending on when customers joined the company. The company should compare strong and weak cohorts to understand what worked better for customers who stayed longer. This can help improve onboarding, support, and long-term retention.



## 9. Payment Failure Risk

Some billing periods had failed payment attempts, but the unresolved failure rate stayed low across all plans. Free had the highest unresolved failure rate at about 1.19%, while Business Monthly was about 1.09% and Enterprise was about 1.01%.

This means payment failure is not the biggest problem compared with churn, but it still affects revenue. The company should continue using payment reminders, retry systems, and clear billing messages so fewer customers lose service because of payment problems.



## 10. Product Usage and Retention

Ended customers had a higher average number of valid usage events than active customers. Ended customers had about 19.54 average usage events, while active customers had about 16.05.

This does not mean that using the product more causes customers to leave. Ended customers may simply have had more time to use the product before they canceled. The company should study usage together with customer lifetime and cancellation reasons to better understand which usage patterns may show that a customer is at risk.



## 11. Final Business Recommendations

The company is growing, but churn is becoming a bigger problem, especially near the end of 2025. The company should focus more on keeping existing customers, especially Enterprise and Business customers because they bring the most revenue.

The company should also review pricing because price was the top cancellation reason. Missing features was another major reason, so improving important product features could also help customers stay longer. The company should give extra attention to industries with higher churn, such as media and nonprofit, and continue investing in strong acquisition channels like organic.

Overall, the best way to improve the business is to reduce churn, protect high-value customers, improve product value, and use customer data to find problems before customers cancel.
