# SaaS Customer Churn & Revenue Analysis

## Business Insights

## 1. Revenue Growth and MRR Trend

Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) grew strongly during the analysis period. It increased from only $237 in January 2023 to a peak of about $887,800 in November 2025.

In December 2025, MRR dropped slightly to about $874,305.

This shows that the company had strong recurring revenue growth, but the small drop at the end of 2025 may be connected to higher churn. The company should watch this trend and focus more on keeping high-value customers.

## 2. Churn Trend

Monthly subscription churn changed throughout the analysis period, but it became higher near the end of 2025.

The churn rate was 7.50% in September 2025, increased to 9.38% in October, 9.73% in November, and reached its highest level of 11.63% in December 2025.

This is an important business problem because higher churn means the company is losing more customers. The company should study why customers are leaving and focus on better onboarding, customer support, product improvements, and early action for customers who may cancel.

## 3. Revenue Concentration by Plan

Enterprise was the strongest revenue-generating plan, producing about $6.27 million in paid invoice value.

Business Annual was second with about $3.41 million, followed by Business Monthly with about $2.17 million.

This shows that a large amount of revenue comes from higher-value plans. The company should focus on keeping Enterprise and Business customers because losing these customers can have a large effect on revenue.

## 4. Cancellation Reasons

Price was the most common cancellation reason at 27.18%.

Missing features was second at 23.77%, followed by no longer needed at 19.24%, switched vendor at 18.92%, and non-payment at 10.89%.

This shows that pricing and product value are two important reasons customers leave. The company should review its pricing and focus on improving the product features customers need the most.

## 5. Revenue at Risk by Plan

Enterprise had the highest monthly recurring revenue at risk from ended subscriptions at about $425,456.

Business Monthly was next at about $169,071, followed by Business Annual at about $123,590.

This shows that losing high-value customers can hurt the company a lot. The company should give more attention to Enterprise and Business customers and try to solve problems before they cancel.

## 6. Acquisition Channel Performance

Organic was the strongest acquisition channel, producing about $4.19 million in paid invoice value.

Paid search was second with about $2.97 million, followed by unknown at about $2.47 million and partner at about $2.14 million.

Partner customers also had a strong average paid value of about $6,513.68 per organization.

This shows that organic brings a large amount of total revenue, while partner customers may also be valuable. The company should continue investing in strong channels and study which channels bring customers who stay longer and spend more.

## 7. Churn by Industry

Media had the highest ended-subscription rate at 61.18%.

Nonprofit was second at 55.92%, followed by government at 54.65% and manufacturing at 54.45%.

Finance had the lowest ended-subscription rate among the industries analyzed at 50.45%.

This shows that some industries lose customers faster than others. The company should study why media and nonprofit customers leave more often and find ways to improve pricing, support, and product value for these groups.

## 8. Cohort Retention

Cohort retention was different between customer signup groups.

Some groups kept more customers after 3, 6, and 12 months, while other groups lost customers faster.

The cohort analysis uses December 31, 2025 as the final analysis cutoff. Subscriptions starting after this date are not included.

Newer cohorts do not have 6-month or 12-month retention values when they have not had enough time to reach those periods.

The company should compare strong and weak cohorts to understand what helped some customers stay longer. This can help improve onboarding, support, and long-term retention.

## 9. Payment Failure Risk

Some billing periods had failed payment attempts, but the unresolved payment failure rate stayed low across all plans.

Free had the highest unresolved failure rate at about 1.19%. Business Monthly was about 1.09%, and Enterprise was about 1.01%.

This shows that payment failure is not the biggest problem compared with customer churn. However, the company should still use payment reminders, retry systems, and clear billing messages to reduce payment problems.

## 10. Product Usage and Retention

Organizations with only ended subscriptions had slightly higher historical product usage than active organizations.

Ended-only organizations had about 18.44 average valid usage events, while active organizations had about 17.33.

Ended-only organizations also had about 76.64 average usage quantity, compared with about 72.09 for active organizations.

This does not mean that higher product usage causes churn. Ended customers may have had more time to use the product before they canceled.

The company should study product usage together with customer lifetime, pricing, plan type, and cancellation reasons before making retention decisions.

## 11. Final Business Recommendations

The company is growing, but customer churn became a bigger problem near the end of 2025.

The company should focus more on keeping Enterprise and Business customers because they bring a large amount of revenue.

Pricing should also be reviewed because price was the biggest cancellation reason. Missing features was the second biggest reason, so improving important product features may also help customers stay longer.

The company should give more attention to industries with high churn, especially media and nonprofit customers.

Organic acquisition should continue to be important because it produced the highest paid invoice value. Partner customers should also be studied because they had a strong average paid value.

The company should also use customer usage, subscription history, plan type, and cancellation data together to find customers who may be at risk.

Overall, the best way to improve the business is to reduce churn, protect high-value customers, improve product value, and find customer problems before they cancel.
